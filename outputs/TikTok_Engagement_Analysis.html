Handling Outliers

count_columns = [
    "video_view_count",
    "video_like_count",
    "video_share_count",
    "video_download_count",
    "video_comment_count"
]

for column in count_columns:
    
    # Calculate Q1 and Q3
    q1 = data[column].quantile(0.25)
    q3 = data[column].quantile(0.75)
    
    # Calculate IQR
    iqr = q3 - q1
    
    # Calculate median
    median = data[column].median()
    
    # Calculate outlier threshold
    threshold = median + (1.5 * iqr)
    
    # Count outliers
    outlier_count = (data[column] > threshold).sum()
    
    # Print result
    print(f"Number of outliers, {column}: {outlier_count}")

Execute

There is a strong positive relationship between video_view_count and video_like_count. As views increase, likes also tend to increase. The data forms a triangular pattern, which suggests engagement varies widely for videos with similar view counts. The first scatterplot shows that claim videos generally reach much higher engagement levels compared to opinion videos. The second plot confirms that opinion videos have significantly lower ranges for both views and likes. The distribution is heavily right-skewed, with a small number of videos receiving extremely high engagement.
